## Findings

### A. Wrong facts, wrong or ambiguous keys (ERROR)

1. **scenes.js, step 8b `vbScene`, bench "Schunk (1982)"**. **ERROR (bar order).** The bars rank four groups: own verbalizations (1) > strategies + own (.85) > model's words (.6) > no verbalizing (.45). The chapter (p. 60) reports only that "self-constructed verbalizations yielded the highest" practice and achievement, and that strategies plus self-constructions gave the highest *self-efficacy*. It gives no order for the other three groups on achievement. Places 2 to 4 are invented, and the second bar also mixes up the self-efficacy winner with the achievement order. **Fix:** draw the winner at full height and the other three at one equal lower height, captioned "order of the others not reported". Or drop the bench values for groups 2 to 4.

2. **scenes.js, step 8b `vbScene`, bench "Schunk & Cox (1986)"**. **ERROR (bar order).** The bars show continuous (1) > discontinued (.55) > none (.45). The chapter (pp. 60–61) says only that "continuous verbalization led to the highest self-efficacy and achievement". It does not order discontinued against none. **Fix:** give discontinued and none equal heights.

3. **scenes.js, step 4b `ipStrat`, round `s4` (Ines: "stops after each section, asks herself who, what and why, and rereads"), key `cm`**. **ERROR (ambiguous key).** The chapter lists *questioning* under elaboration too (p. 50: learners "stop periodically as they read and ask themselves questions") as well as under comprehension monitoring (p. 51: "stop periodically while reading and ask themselves questions (who, what, when, where, why, how)"). A student who taps "Elaboration" has a defensible answer. **Fix:** make the card clearly about checking understanding, e.g. "...asks herself who, what and why *to check whether she has understood*, and rereads when she cannot answer". Add one line to `say`: "Questioning also appears under elaboration, where the aim is to add meaning; here the aim is to check understanding."

4. **scenes.js, step 6b `cyScene`, round `c6` (Kim, "during a short pause... notices that her method is too slow and thinks about why"), key `sr`**. **ERROR (ambiguous key).** "Notices that her method is too slow" is monitoring, which the chapter puts in performance control (p. 56: "monitoring and recording one's performances"). Only the pause and the "thinks about why" (attribution) point to self-reflection. **Fix:** remove the monitoring element, e.g. "Halfway through, Kim stops for a short break, judges how far she has got and explains why it is going slowly."

5. **scenes.js, step 9a `srsdScene`, rounds `s3` (key `mo`) vs `s4` (key `su`)**. **ERROR (ambiguous key).** The s3 card says "The teacher writes a story *with the class*", which reads like the collaborative writing of "Support it". ### B. Overstatements and our synthesis presented as the chapter's claim (OVERSTATED / UNSUPPORTED)

9. **body.html step 9 tab ② "So..." paragraph ("It is also the chapter's strongest evidence, because it is a randomized experiment..."); scenes.js `refScene` endOut ("It is the chapter's strongest evidence that self-regulation can be taught"); body.html Comprehension check Q3 ("Which study in the chapter gives the strongest evidence...")**. **OVERSTATED.** The chapter never ranks evidence. It only notes that this intervention "was a true experiment involving random assignment" and that the effects were "causal" and "large or near large" (p. 63). Many other studies in the chapter are experiments too (Cleary et al.; the Schunk studies). **Fix:** "The chapter stresses that this was a true experiment with random assignment, so the effects can be read as causal, and they were large or near large." Reword Q3 to "Why can the chapter interpret the effects of Zimmerman et al. (2011) as causal?"
