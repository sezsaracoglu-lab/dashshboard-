Theme 3 · Put it together

🧩 Compare the theories, then use them on a case

Each reading teaches one piece: reading 1 gives five theories and the research, reading 2 shows self-regulation inside one skill (reading), and reading 3 works out one theory in depth. This page lays the pieces next to each other, organized by the five questions the theme asks of you.

1 · How is self-regulated learning defined, and what are its key components?

One idea, three ways of describing it

The definitions and components come from the readings. Putting them in one table, and the row "the common core", are ours.

ReadingHow it defines self-regulationThe key components it names
📘 Reading 1
Schunk & Zimmerman (2012)		Learning that results from a student's own thoughts and behaviours, systematically aimed at reaching her learning goals (Zimmerman, 2000)Three features shared by all theories: the learner is active (behaviourally, metacognitively, motivationally); the process is cyclical (goal, monitor, respond); and the theories ask why people regulate, so motivation and emotion are regulated too
📗 Reading 2
Thiede & de Bruin (2017)		An interplay of metacognitive monitoring and regulation of study: the reader tracks how well she understands and rereads until she judges the text masteredMonitoring (judging comprehension) and control (choosing what to restudy); the loop depends on the accuracy of monitoring
📙 Reading 3
Usher & Schunk (2017)		Systematically organizing one's thoughts, feelings and actions to attain one's goalsThree subfunctions (self-observation, self-evaluation, self-reaction); three phases (forethought, performance, reflection); six interacting components (cognition, behaviour, motivation, emotion, social, environment); self-efficacy as foundation
⭐ The common core
our summary		A goal, monitoring of where you stand, a response that closes the gap (strategy, effort, help, restudy), and the motivation and belief to keep the loop going. Reading 1's definition is the broadest; reading 2 zooms in on the monitoring link; reading 3 adds who you are (self-efficacy, emotions) and where you are (social, environment).
2 · How do the five theoretical perspectives explain self-regulated learning, and what are their limitations?

Five lenses on the same learner

The first three columns come from reading 1 (Table 3.1 and its sections) and, for social cognitive theory, from reading 3. The last column separates two things: limits the readings state (marked 📖), and what each lens leaves in the background, which is our reading (marked ✎) and not a claim of the authors.

TheoryHow it explains self-regulationKey processesResemblesLimits
🎯 Operant
Skinner		Choosing among courses of action, typically by deferring an immediate reinforcer for a later, usually greater oneSelf-monitoring, self-instruction, self-reinforcementSelf-monitoring is substantially similar to self-observation in social cognitive theory; ✎ self-reinforcement resembles self-reaction (our link)📖 Focusing only on behaviour, it ignores cognitive and motivational aspects (beliefs, emotions); effective in the short term, but long-term goals need a more elaborate view. 📖 It is unclear whether self-reinforcement works better than reinforcement from others.
🧠 Information processingSelf-regulation is roughly metacognition: monitoring, directing and regulating actions toward goals; a system compares the present state with a standard and reduces the gap (TOTE; Winne and Hadwin)Metacognitive knowledge and monitoring; learning strategies (rehearsal, elaboration, organization, comprehension monitoring)The compare-and-fix loop is the same loop reading 2 studies in reading; Winne and Hadwin include motivational variables✎ The lens puts the mind's machinery in the foreground; the social world and development stay in the background (our reading).
🌱 DevelopmentalSelf-regulation grows with age as cognitive processes improve; speech gradually comes to direct behaviourPhases of self-control (Kopp); private speech (Luria); strategy deficienciesShares private speech with Vygotsky and metacognition with information processing📖 Research on private speech found its developmental pattern more complex than originally hypothesized by Vygotsky.
🏗️ Social constructivist
Vygotsky		Learners construct theories about themselves, tasks and strategies with cultural tools, in social contexts and the zone of proximal developmentPrivate speech and internalization; zone of proximal development; implicit theories (mindsets)Scaffolding that fades resembles the social-self progression; mindsets return in reading 3 as implicit theory of ability✎ The lens says how learners build self-regulation with others, less how it runs moment to moment in a task (our reading).
🔺 Social cognitive
Bandura, Zimmerman		Reciprocal interaction of person, behaviour and environment; a cycle of forethought, performance and reflection, powered by self-efficacySelf-observation, self-evaluation, self-reaction; goals; models; social-self progressionIntegrates pieces of the other four: monitoring, private speech (self-talk), scaffolded internalization📖 The social-self progression is not claimed to be the only route. 📖 Research has mostly used university and middle-class high school samples (reading 3). ✎ Its own condition, that self-observation alone is not reliably related to self-regulation, shows how many links must be in place (our reading).
Reading 1 does not crown one theory: it says each perspective has important implications for research and practice. It has a formal critique section only for operant theory; elsewhere it notes mixed research findings. It also warns, for self-regulation research in general, that assuming it works the same in every culture is unwarranted. Reading 3 presents the social cognitive view as the authors' own framework. 3 · Which instructional practices follow from each theory?

From theory to practice, with the condition that makes it work

The practices and conditions come from the readings; the arrangement by theory is ours.

🎯 OperantSet up cues (a fixed time and place), remove distracting cues, self-record behaviour, self-instruction statements, self-reward (Brad's plan)Two criteria for self-monitoring: regularity (keep a continual record) and proximity (record close in time to the behaviour). Explicit reinforcement may matter more than self-reinforcement while skills are being learned.Reading 1, step 3

4 · How can self-regulated learning be applied in reading?

A self-regulating reader, before, during and after

Placing the readings' techniques on Zimmerman's three phases is our arrangement; each technique and its evidence comes from the reading named.

PhaseWhat the reader doesWhy it worksSource
🔭 Before readingKnow what kind of test is coming (deep comprehension or facts); set a goal for the textExpecting inference tests improved metacomprehension accuracy; expecting memory tests improved metamemory accuracyReading 2 (Thiede et al., 2011)
📖 While readingSelf-question (who, what, when, where, why, how); paraphrase; check consistency; self-explain each sentence; make your own concept mapMonitoring activities enhanced understanding (Greene & Azevedo, 2009); self-explanation and generated maps build the situation model and improve accuracyReading 1, step 4 · Reading 2, step 5
⏳ After a delayWrite keywords or a summary, or complete a cause-and-effect diagram, then judge how well you understood each textDelayed retrieval gives cues that tell well and poorly understood texts apartReading 2, step 4
🔁 Then regulateReread the texts you judged less well understoodAccurate judgments lead to better restudy choices and better comprehension (Thiede et al., 2003)Reading 2, step 6

The warning that runs through reading 2: readers already regulate well (they reread what seems less learned), but they monitor badly, often judging from surface cues such as how easy the text felt. So the work is on monitoring.

5 · How can educational professionals support the development of self-regulated learning?

What the three readings tell a teacher

The advice is the readings'; grouping it into four moves is ours.

MoveWhat to doSource
👀 Show, then hand overModel the strategy and the self-statements, practise together with feedback, then fade support until students work alone and adapt (observation, emulation, self-control, self-regulation); SRSD's five stages do this for writingReading 1, steps 6 and 9 · Reading 3, step 5

6 · Use it on a case

The case clinic: diagnose first, then choose the move

🎯 Five students, two decisions each. First decide what is going on (the diagnosis), then what a teacher could do (the treatment). The students are our invention; the diagnoses and moves come from the readings.

How to answer a case question. Ask three things in order. What is the symptom? (what the student does or says). What is the diagnosis? (which part of the loop is failing: the goal, the monitoring, the response, the belief, or the hand-over from outside control to self-control). What is the treatment? (the practice from the readings that repairs that part, with its condition).

A real student often shows more than one problem at once. Then name the link that fails first: in reading 2's logic, regulation cannot do better than the monitoring it relies on.
