How does a baby who controls almost nothing become a child who can steer herself?

Why this matters. Operant and information processing theory describe how self-regulation works. Developmental theory asks how it grows. That tells a teacher what to expect from a learner of a given age. As you scroll, the timeline at the top fills in.

What is self-regulation here?

⭐ It grows: as children develop, their thinking changes in ways that give them more and more control over their thoughts, feelings and actions.

Control means starting and stopping an action, waiting before acting on a goal, and behaving in ways others approve of (Kopp, 1982). In the first months there is hardly any: from 0 to 3 months the baby controls only her arousal and simple acts such as reaching; from 3 to 9 months her behaviour adjusts to what happens around her.

When does the first real control appear?

⭐ At 9 to 12 months, when the baby does what a caregiver asks: compliance.

It is the first voluntary control. It depends on how receptive she is and on the quality of the mother-child relationship. The control is still outside her: someone else says "stop".

When does control move inside?

⭐ In the second year: impulse control, the critical shift from outside to inside control.

At 12 to 18 months she notices what a situation demands and can start, continue and stop actions herself. Parents discipline more, and her compliance becomes linked to internalizing rules: taking them in as her own (Kochanska et al., 1998).

What do language and thought add?

⭐ Language lets words steer her (self-control, 24 to 36 months); then thought steers her and she adapts to new demands (self-regulation, from 36 months).

So this means: self-regulation is the end of a long path from outside control to inside control.
