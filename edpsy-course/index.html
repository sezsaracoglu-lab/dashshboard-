Educational Psychology · Start here

How do people learn effectively, and how do you design for it?

This course asks how to make sure people learn well, stay motivated, get instruction that fits their abilities, collaborate successfully and are assessed fairly, at school and at work. Educational psychologists answer these questions with knowledge from cognitive, developmental and social psychology. Every week you meet one real case, in one professional role, and use the readings to solve it.

How the course works

One case a week, one professional role, the readings as your toolkit

Each week you read articles or book chapters on one topic. In the Friday tutorial you work in a small group on assignments that apply the readings to a case, and present your solution. Discussing how theories can be applied, and how interpretations differ, is where the real understanding happens.

🧠 How do you study so it sticks?

Role: designer of a learning tool

Many students choose study methods that give only short-term, surface learning. Your team must design an app that teaches learners how to study: engaging, scientifically grounded, and good for deep learning and long-term retention. Three articles give you three design rules: how learners should engage, which strategy suits which age, and when to study.

Big idea: learning lasts when the learner does the mental work: generating something new, at the right level for their age, and retrieving it after a gap.
  • M1-1Chi & Wylie (2014): the ICAP framework, Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive
  • M1-2Brod (2021): which generative learning strategies work at which age
  • M1-3Gerbier & Toppino (2015): distributed practice and why spacing beats cramming

What you should be able to do at the end

The four course goals

1

Recognise the different professional roles an educational psychologist can have in practice.

2

Understand and know the main theories, principles and frameworks on motivation, self-regulation, memory strategies, learning problems, assessment, technology, training and transfer.

3

Apply theories, principles and frameworks to real-world problems in education.

4

Compare and connect theories, principles and frameworks in the context of real-world problems.

Why it matters beyond school: organisational psychologists use it for training and motivation at work, clinical psychologists for learning difficulties and self-regulation, and Brain and Cognition students to connect memory and attention research with real learning.
The course so far in one sentence

People learn best when they do the mental work, with strategies that fit who they are, at the right moment. The coming themes will add motivation, self-regulation, assessment, technology and transfer.
