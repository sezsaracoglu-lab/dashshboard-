Green human resource practices and organizational citizenship behavior for the environment

Luu, T. T. (2019). Green human resource practices and organizational citizenship behavior for the environment: The roles of collective green crafting and environmentally specific servant leadership. Journal of Sustainable Tourism, 27(8), 1167–1196.

STEP1
Who wrote it, and what he wants to know

How and when do green HR practices lead to voluntary green behavior?

❓ This step answers: What is the research question, and what does the study add to earlier research?
🔎 Take the question apart: four short decisions. Each one fills a box of the design.
Author
Trong Tuan Luu, Swinburne Business School, Swinburne University of Technology, Australia
Type
Empirical article in the Journal of Sustainable Tourism (2019): a survey study that tests seven hypotheses
Question
How and when do green HR practices contribute to organizational citizenship behavior for the environment, of individuals and of teams?
Main message
Green HR practices go with more voluntary green behavior, partly because teams craft their green work together, and more strongly under an environmentally specific servant leader

Some words first. HR means human resources: the part of an organization that deals with its people; HRM is human resource management. Voluntary or extra-role behavior is what you do without it being in your job description. An empirical study collects data. A hypothesis is a prediction that the data can support or not. In the picture: four boxes that are still empty: a cause, a link in between, a condition above, and an outcome.

The article begins with tourism: because of its growth and high carbon intensity, tourism will form a growing part of the world's greenhouse gas emissions. Green policies and strategies can reduce the carbon footprint of tourism organizations. But a strategy has to be translated into what employees do, and the author sees HR practices as the translator: green HR practices align selection, training and performance assessment with green objectives. Sustainability at the macro level starts with individual actions.

Three gaps. Tourism research had looked at corporate social responsibility far more than at green HR practices. Research on green behavior had stayed at the level of the individual, although teams matter for carrying out a strategy. And two things had been studied separately but not together: green HRM and leadership. The article's own Table 1 lists the key earlier studies; none examined the interaction of green HR practices with leadership, or a team-level crafting mechanism.

The author names several contributions. The study adds team-level voluntary green behavior as an outcome, next to the individual level. It brings evidence from tourism in a South-East Asian emerging market, where earlier work focused on manufacturing or on other services such as hospitality and education. It uses conservation of resources theory to explain how and when the practices work, with a new mediator, collective green crafting, which joins HRM research and job crafting research. And it examines the interaction between green HR practices and a leadership style that was new to this field: environmentally specific servant leadership.

⚠ The trap✗ The study is about whether green HR practices make employees do their green tasks better.✓ The outcome is voluntary green behavior: citizenship behavior for the environment, which is not specified in the job description.
📝 Exam-style questionIn this study, which variable answers the "how" question and which the "when" question? Name the technical term for each role.

Model answer: "How" is answered by collective green crafting, which is the mediator: the mechanism through which green HR practices relate to organizational citizenship behavior for the environment. "When" is answered by environmentally specific servant leadership, which is the moderator: it strengthens the relationships of green HR practices with collective green crafting and with citizenship behavior at team and individual level.

STEP2
Key concepts and definitions · Figure 1

Five concepts, one research model

❓ This step answers: What is green human resource management, what is organizational citizenship behavior for the environment, and what are the other concepts in the model?
🧩 Read five questionnaire items: each one measures one concept. Tap the box in the model where it belongs.

Organizational citizenship behavior is helpful behavior beyond your formal job. OCBE is the short form the article uses for organizational citizenship behavior for the environment. Collective means: of the team as a whole. Job crafting, from theme 1, is employees changing their own job demands and job resources. An item is one sentence in a questionnaire that people rate. In the picture: the research model with five empty boxes. Five items appear one by one; tap the box each item measures.

OCBE. Green behavior is treated as a form of organizational citizenship behavior. It mirrors an employee's willingness to work with the organization and its members on behaviors above and beyond the job role that benefit the natural environment. The author extends this to the team: collective OCBE. He follows the three dimensions of Boiral and Paillé (2012), which you met in reading 1 as one of the citizenship scales: eco-civic engagement, eco-helping and eco-initiatives.

Green HR practices are designed to carry out the organization's HRM strategy with a green aim. They consist of recruitment and selection, training, performance management, rewarding and involvement, and they aim to build green values as well as knowledge and skills for green activities. Details per practice are in the scene. One point the article stresses: green training should also aim at attitudes and emotional involvement, and integrated training links the programs to performance management, which helps to create a green climate.

Collective green crafting rests on the idea that employees are more motivated when they can redesign their job in terms of tasks and relationships and experience it as more meaningful. Jobs can be crafted individually and collectively. When team members interact and craft resources together in the direction of the team's green goals, that is collective green crafting: more structural and relational resources, more challenging demands, fewer hindering demands, all for green work.

Environmentally specific servant leadership starts from servant leadership: leading with the motivation to serve others, being a role model with empathy and altruistic values, and feeling a moral responsibility for the organization, its employees and the community. The author argues that this style may suit green behavior well, because environmental psychologists suggest that altruistic, other-oriented reasons may bring about green behavior, and because leader conscientiousness and moral reflectiveness have been linked to voluntary workplace green behavior. Open "Servant versus other leadership styles" at the end of the scene for the comparison.

⚠ The trap✗ Collective OCBE is the average of how much voluntary green behavior each team member shows.✓ The article defines it as the synergy of members' efforts in green activities, not the sum of individual contributions. In the study it was rated for the team as a whole, by the manager.
📝 Exam-style questionA hotel reception team decides together to learn about waste separation, asks the kitchen team for feedback, and takes on a towel-reuse project. Which concept is this, and which three parts do you recognise?

Model answer: Collective green crafting: team members combining efforts to change the resources and demands of their green work. Learning about waste separation increases structural job resources (knowledge and skills for green activities). Asking the kitchen team for feedback increases social (relational) job resources. Taking on the towel-reuse project increases challenging job demands through a new green project. The fourth part, not shown here, is reducing hindering job demands.

STEP3
The theory behind the predictions

Conservation of resources: who has, invests

❓ This step answers: Why would green HR practices lead to voluntary green behavior, and what are the seven hypotheses?
🎒 Reason like the author: six cases. Each answer lights up the hypotheses it supports.

Resources, in this theory, are personal attributes, energies or conditions that a person values: knowledge, skills, support, self-esteem. Conservation of resources theory (Hobfoll) says people try to keep, protect and build resources; you met it in theme 1. Proactive means acting on your own initiative. In the picture: two situations at the top (ample or limited resources) and three rows of hypotheses that light up as you solve the cases.

The theory has two sides. A lack or loss of resources may have negative psychological effects, so people try to avoid loss; with limited resources they turn defensive and perform at the minimum. With ample resources they take a proactive resource gain strategy: they go above and beyond minimum expectations with proactive, voluntary or extra-role behavior. Such behavior can be seen as an exchange of resources for resource gain spirals in structural resources (knowledge and skills), social resources (relationships) and personal resources (self-esteem and pride). And resources can change form: the resource caravan.

Hypotheses 1 and 2. Green HR practices are organizational-level resources. Training gives green values, knowledge and skills; communication channels keep employees informed; involvement gives opportunities for problem-solving on environmental issues. The practices also create a green learning atmosphere and a norm for behaving pro-environmentally and helping each other do so. With these resources, team members are expected to engage in OCBE individually and collectively. Earlier evidence pointed the same way for green behavior, extra-role green behavior and green creativity.

Hypotheses 3 and 4. Employees are active players. When they perceive ample organizational resources for green activities, they are motivated to build more, and one way is collective green crafting. Crafting then feeds OCBE: for the team through a stronger norm, person-group fit and shared values; for the individual through shared knowledge, peer coaching and feedback, observational learning from more role models, and open communication that helps turn knowledge into initiatives.

Hypotheses 5 to 7. Leadership has been found to moderate the effects of HRM practices. Environmentally specific servant leaders are role models who reinforce the green norm that HR practices cultivate; followers spread these values through the team in a contagion process; managers act as gatekeepers for the norm. Because managers often give less support to green behavior than to other management activities, leadership and HR practices are not equivalent sources of resources: the leader adds to the HR practices and does not replace them.

⚠ The trap✗ In this study employees show green citizenship behavior to pay the organization back for good treatment.✓ That is the social exchange explanation, which the author deliberately does not use. His explanation is conservation of resources: people with ample resources invest them to gain more.
📝 Exam-style questionA company cuts all green training and stops informing staff about its environmental plans. Predict with conservation of resources theory what happens to voluntary green behavior, and name the strategy.

Model answer: Voluntary green behavior is expected to drop. Green training and information are organizational-level resources. Without them, employees possess limited green-related resources and are prone to a defensive resource strategy: they protect or conserve their remaining resources and perform at the minimum level, so they do not invest in behavior above and beyond minimum expectations such as OCBE or green crafting. With ample resources they would take a proactive resource gain strategy.

STEP4
Research method

Two questionnaires, two months apart, employees and their managers

❓ This step answers: How was the study carried out, and what does that allow us to conclude?
🧪 Be the researcher: six design decisions. Choose what you would do; the timeline fills in with what the study did.

A survey collects answers to questionnaires. A wave is one round of data collection. A sample is the group of people who took part. A scale is a set of items that together measure one concept. A control variable is something measured so that its influence can be taken out. In the picture: a timeline with two moments and four empty boxes.

Who and where. Tour operators based in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, with at least 100 employees, an established green strategy, and tour departments of at least ten employees. The author used his connections and asked chief executives to introduce other operators (snowball sampling, which is not a random sample). Employees and their direct managers in the tour departments were invited by telephone and received different questionnaires by e-mail; only employees who had worked under their current manager for at least one year were contacted.

What was measured. Five scales, all answered on five points: green HR practices (6 items; Dumont and colleagues, 2017), collective green crafting (8 items adapted from the collective job crafting scale of Tims and colleagues, 2013; how often the team did each thing), environmentally specific servant leadership (12 items), collective OCBE (10 items adapted from Boiral and Paillé, 2012; rated by managers) and individual OCBE (10 items; Boiral and Paillé, 2012). Response: 71% of the invited employees at time 1; the final sample was 51% of the employees and 68% of the managers.

How it was analysed. With multilevel structural equation modeling: a technique that estimates all paths of a model at once and respects that employees are nested in teams. Mediation was tested through the indirect effect with bootstrapped confidence intervals; moderation through interaction terms and simple slopes. The numbers behind the checks are in the card "The checks, for the curious".

⚠ The trap✗ Because the survey had two waves, the study shows that green HR practices cause green crafting.✓ Two waves separate the measurements in time, which helps against common method bias. But it was not a cross-lagged study, and the author states that causal relationships might not be derived.
📝 Exam-style questionName two things the study did to reduce common method bias, and one reason why its measure of green HR practices may still differ from reality.

Model answer: Against common method bias (any two): a two-month time lag between the measurement of the independent variables and of the mediator and outcomes; collective OCBE rated by direct managers and not by the employees; a marker variable test. Why the measure may differ from reality: green HR practices were measured as employees' perceptions, and the author notes that data collected through perceptions may not reflect what actually happens in the workplace; he suggests reports from HR departments for future research.

STEP5
Results · Tables 3 and 4 · Figures 2 to 4

All seven hypotheses supported

❓ This step answers: Do green HR practices go with more voluntary green behavior, and does collective green crafting carry that link?
📊 Predict, then read the numbers: five results. Each answer writes the path coefficients into the model.

A path coefficient says how strongly one variable in the model predicts another: zero means not at all, a higher positive number a stronger positive link. Significant means the link is unlikely to be chance. A confidence interval is the range in which the true value probably lies; if zero is outside it, the effect counts as real. Post hoc means an extra analysis that was not one of the hypotheses. In the picture: the model with question marks on the arrows.

Before the model, look at the plain correlations (Table 3, below). On the five-point scales, the averages sit around the middle to upper half: 3.51 for green HR practices, 3.47 for collective green crafting, 3.44 for collective OCBE, 3.49 for individual OCBE and 3.39 for leadership. Green HR practices correlate .43 with collective green crafting, .36 with collective OCBE and .38 with individual OCBE. The control variables (age, gender, education, tenure, team size) hardly correlate with anything.

In the model, green HR practices showed significant positive associations with collective OCBE and with individual OCBE: hypotheses 1 and 2. The author notes this is in line with an earlier report of a positive link between green HR practices and extra-role green behavior (Dumont and colleagues, 2017).

The mediation: green HR practices went with more collective green crafting, and collective green crafting with more OCBE at both levels. The indirect effects were significant, and a model with partial mediation fitted better than one with full mediation: hypotheses 3 and 4. Two findings were not predicted: collective OCBE was positively related to individual OCBE, and the leadership style itself was positively related to both kinds of OCBE.

📝 Exam-style questionThe indirect effect of green HR practices on individual OCBE through collective green crafting is .17, with a 95% confidence interval from .04 to .29. What do you conclude, and why?

Model answer: The indirect effect is significant, because the confidence interval does not contain zero. This supports hypothesis 4: collective green crafting mediates the positive relationship between green HR practices and individual OCBE. Because the partial mediation model fitted better than the full mediation model, crafting carries part of the relationship and a direct relationship remains.

❓ This step answers: Does environmentally specific servant leadership change how strongly green HR practices work?
📈 Read three interaction figures: predict the first one, then interpret what you see.

An interaction figure shows the effect of one variable separately for groups that differ on a second variable. Here: the horizontal axis is green HR practices (low to high), the vertical axis is the outcome, and there are two lines: teams whose leader is high on environmentally specific servant leadership (one standard deviation above the mean) and teams whose leader is low (one standard deviation below). If the lines have different steepness, the leader changes the effect of the HR practices.

The same result came out three times. For collective green crafting, for collective OCBE and for individual OCBE, the line was clearly steeper with high leadership, and still rose with low leadership. The interaction terms were all significantly positive, and the models that included the interaction fitted better than those without. The author links this to earlier findings on interactions between leadership and organizational support for the environment, and between this leadership style and corporate social responsibility initiatives.

For practice this is the strongest message of the article: HR practices and leaders have to say the same thing. Compare it with reading 2, where a leader's "moderating action" was the pathway by which a leader strengthens or weakens what the organization's policies do to followers. This study can be read as an example of that pathway, measured with a leadership style (our link).

⚠ The trap✗ Without a green-serving leader, green HR practices have no effect.✓ The slopes with low leadership (.22, .15, .17) are smaller but still positive and significant. The leader strengthens the effect; the effect does not depend on the leader entirely.
📝 Exam-style questionDescribe in words what Figure 4 shows, using the two simple slopes.

Model answer: Figure 4 shows the relationship between green HR practices and individual OCBE for employees whose leader is high versus low on environmentally specific servant leadership. In both groups individual OCBE rises as green HR practices rise, but it rises more steeply when leadership is high (simple slope .63) than when it is low (simple slope .17). The lines are therefore not parallel: leadership moderates, and more precisely strengthens, the positive relationship. This supports hypothesis 7.

The author's conclusion (p. 1186), in our words. Green HR practices were positively related to organizational citizenship behavior for the environment of individuals and of teams, with collective green crafting as the mediator. Environmentally specific servant leadership made the positive relationships of green HR practices with collective green crafting and with citizenship behavior at both levels stronger.

🧰 What organizations should do, according to the author

Implement green HR practices to reach the green goals. Through green training, give employees green-related resources: awareness and values, other-oriented values, and the knowledge and skills for green activities and eco-initiatives such as eco tours or destination greening programs. Include employee involvement activities that grant team members autonomy to act as proactive agents of the green agenda and to craft resources for green activities together. Ensure consistency in signals from HR practices and managers. Build environmentally specific servant leadership across the organization through leadership training and succession planning, and let managers share their experience with it in training sessions or through internal communication.

⚠️ Limitations the author names

No cross-lagged design, so causal relationships might not be derived. Data based on participants' perceptions, which may not reflect what actually happens; future studies could use HR department reports for the practices and participant observation for OCBE. Self-reported questionnaires carry a risk of common method bias, although the author judges it not a serious concern here because of the marker variable test, the moderation tests and the two sources. Results from tourism in Vietnam may not generalize to other industries and markets; the collectivistic nature of Vietnamese culture might influence the degree of team green behavior, so the model should be compared in collectivistic and individualistic cultures.

🧩 The meeting's questions, from this reading

What is green human resource management? HR activities that enhance positive environmental outcomes: recruitment and selection, training, performance management, rewards and involvement, aligned with green objectives. What is organizational citizenship behavior for the environment? Voluntary behavior, not in the job description, that helps make the organization or society more sustainable; for a team, the synergy of members' voluntary green efforts; in three forms: eco-civic engagement, eco-helping and eco-initiatives. How can organizations increase pro-environmental behaviors? This study's answer: green HR practices, room for teams to craft their green work, and leaders who send the same green signal. For the group assignment on a company-wide initiative, those are its three building blocks: policies, involvement and incentives for employees, and leadership.
