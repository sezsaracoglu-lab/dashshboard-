Five concepts, one research model

❓ This step answers: What is green human resource management, what is organizational citizenship behavior for the environment, and what are the other concepts in the model?

What is green human resource management, what is organizational citizenship behavior for the environment, and what are the other concepts in the model?Why this matters. Figure 1 has five boxes. You need each concept sharp before the hypotheses make sense. An item is one sentence in a questionnaire that people rate.In this step you learn:1OCBE, individual and collective2Green HR practices3Collective green crafting4Environmentally specific servant leadership

⭐ Voluntary behavior that helps the natural environment and goes beyond the job role; here measured for individuals and for teams.Organizational citizenship behavior is helpful behavior beyond your formal job; OCBE is its environmental form (Daily and colleagues, 2009). The author extends it to the team: collective OCBE, the synergy of members' efforts, not the sum. He follows the three dimensions of Boiral and Paillé (2012), one of the citizenship scales in reading 1: eco-civic engagement (taking part voluntarily in the organization's green programs), eco-helping (helping others take environmental concerns into their work) and eco-initiatives (own suggestions and actions to improve green performance).So this means: an exam option calling collective OCBE the average of members' behavior is wrong: it is their synergy, rated by the manager.→ Next: the HR side.

⭐ Recruitment and selection, training, performance management, rewarding and involvement aimed at green values, knowledge and skills.HR practices carry out an organization's HRM strategy; the green ones aim to build green values and the knowledge and skills for green activities. Selection screens for green awareness; training builds awareness, knowledge, skills and a green climate; performance management assesses green performance and gives feedback; rewards are financial and non-financial (green travel benefits, green recognition); involvement offers a clear green vision, learning and communication channels. The article stresses that green training should also aim at attitudes and emotional involvement, and that integrated training links programs to performance management, which helps create a green climate.So this means: green HR is a bundle across the whole employee cycle.→ Next: the team mechanism.

⭐ Team members together changing the resources and demands of their green work, to make it more meaningful and reach the team's green goals.Job crafting (theme 1) is employees changing their own demands and resources; jobs can be crafted individually or collectively. Collective green crafting means more structural and relational resources, more challenging demands (demands that stretch you, like a new green project) and fewer hindering demands (demands that get in the way), all for green work. Our example: a tour team that together redesigns its trips to cut waste and teaches each other how.So this means: crafting becomes a team activity aimed at green goals.→ Next: the leader.

⭐ A leader who is a role model of green values and commitment to green goals, and who serves and helps employees contribute to the sustainable growth of the organization and the community (Luu, 2018).General servant leadership means leading with the motivation to serve others, as an empathic role model with altruistic values and a moral responsibility for the organization, employees and community. The author argues it may suit green behavior well: environmental psychologists suggest altruistic, other-oriented reasons may bring about green behavior, and leader conscientiousness and moral reflectiveness have been linked to voluntary green behavior.So this means: a leader who serves others may model the other-oriented motive behind green behavior.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

STEP3
The theory behind the predictions

Conservation of resources: who has, invests

❓ This step answers: Why would green HR practices lead to voluntary green behavior, and what are the seven hypotheses?

Why would green HR practices lead to voluntary green behavior, and what are the seven hypotheses?Why this matters. The study explains its predictions with conservation of resources theory (Hobfoll), which you met in theme 1. Resources here are personal attributes, energies or conditions people value: knowledge, skills, support, self-esteem.In this step you learn:1The theory's logic: who has, invests2Hypotheses 1 and 2: the direct links3Hypotheses 3 and 4: crafting as the route4Hypotheses 5 to 7: the leader as amplifier

⭐ People with ample resources invest them proactively to gain more; people with few resources turn defensive.A lack or loss of resources may harm people, so they avoid loss and, with limited resources, perform at the minimum. With ample resources they take a proactive resource gain strategy: going above and beyond with voluntary, extra-role behavior. Bordia and colleagues (2017) see this as a trade: resources in, growing resources back (resource gain spirals), structural (knowledge, skills), social (relationships) and personal (self-esteem, pride). Resources can also change form, from structural or social into personal resources such as pride: the resource caravan.So this means: an exam option saying the study's main explanation is social exchange (paying back) is wrong: the main explanation is investing resources; the author offers it as an alternative that does not challenge social exchange theory.→ Next: the first hypotheses.

⭐ Because green HR practices are organizational resources: knowledge, skills, information, chances to solve problems, and a green norm (H1 team, H2 individual).Training gives green values, knowledge and skills; communication keeps employees informed; involvement offers problem-solving on environmental issues. The practices also create a green learning atmosphere and a norm for behaving pro-environmentally and helping each other. With these resources, members are expected to show OCBE individually and collectively.So this means: more green HR, more voluntary green behavior, of teams and individuals.→ Next: the route.

⭐ Because resources from HR motivate teams to build more resources together, and that shared crafting then makes green behavior the team norm and easier to learn (H3 team, H4 individual).Why HR leads to crafting: employees are active players, not passive receivers of HR. Green training gives knowledge and skills, and supervisor feedback gives support; with these, teams take on challenging new green projects (new green tours, greening destinations) and share the load to cut hindering demands. Crafting is the mediator: the route through which HR works. For the team: a stronger norm, person-group fit (how well a person matches the team's values) and shared values. For the individual: shared knowledge, peer coaching and feedback, observational learning from more role models, and open communication that turns knowledge into initiatives.So this means: HR works partly by getting teams to reshape their green work together.→ Next: the leader.

⭐ Because such leaders model and reinforce the green norm that HR cultivates, and spread it through the team (H5 to H7).The prediction: the links of green HR with team crafting, collective OCBE and individual OCBE are stronger when followers see their leader as high on environmentally specific servant leadership. Employees react more strongly to HR's green signals when the leader sends the same signal (Ostroff & Bowen, 2016), and hold back when they doubt the manager's real motive (Chan and colleagues, 2014). Leadership has been found to moderate HRM effects; such leaders are role models, followers spread their values (a contagion process), and managers act as gatekeepers. Because managers often support green behavior less than other activities, leader and HR are not equivalent: the leader adds to the HR practices rather than replacing them.So this means: the leader is an amplifier of what HR starts.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Conservation of resources: green HR gives resources that people invest in voluntary green behavior (H1, H2), partly through collective crafting (H3, H4), more strongly under a green-serving leader (H5 to H7).1Theory? Who has resources, invests them proactively.2H1, H2? Green HR → collective and individual OCBE.3H3, H4? Through collective green crafting.4H5–H7? Leader strengthens the links.Typical exam questions on this step:?Which theory does the author mainly use? (conservation of resources)?What is the mediator in the model? (collective green crafting)

⚠ The trap✗ The study explains green citizenship behavior with social exchange theory: employees pay the organization back for good treatment.✓ Paying back is the social exchange explanation. The author's main explanation is conservation of resources: people with ample resources invest them to gain more. He offers it as an alternative that does not challenge social exchange theory, and notes that investing in OCBE is also in line with an exchange of resources with the organization.
STEP4
Research method

Two questionnaires, two months apart, employees and their managers

❓ This step answers: How was the study carried out, and what does that allow us to conclude?

How was the study carried out, and what does that allow us to conclude?Why this matters. A finding is only as strong as its design. This study used two waves and two sources, which helps, but it is still a survey. A wave is one round of data collection; a sample is the group of people who took part.In this step you learn:1The sample2The measures3The analysis4What the design allows

⭐ Employees and their direct managers in tour departments of Vietnamese tour operators with an established green strategy.Operators based in Ho Chi Minh City with at least 100 employees and tour departments of at least ten employees. The author used his connections and asked chief executives to introduce other operators: snowball sampling, not a random sample. Only employees who had worked under their manager for at least a year were contacted; employees and managers got different questionnaires by e-mail.So this means: the sample fits the question, but it is not representative of all organizations.→ Next: the measures.

⭐ Five scales on five-point answers, with collective OCBE rated by managers and the rest by employees.1Green HR practices: 6 items (Dumont and colleagues, 2017).2Collective green crafting: 8 items adapted from Tims and colleagues (2013).3Environmentally specific servant leadership: 12 items (Luu, 2018, adapted from Liden and colleagues, 2008).4Collective OCBE: 10 items adapted from Boiral & Paillé (2012), rated by managers.5Individual OCBE: 10 items (Boiral & Paillé, 2012).Two waves, two months apart, from December 2017: at time 1, employees rated green HR practices and their leader; at time 2, the same employees rated collective green crafting and their own OCBE, and managers rated collective OCBE. Response: 71% of invited employees at time 1; the final sample was 51% of employees and 68% of managers: 1,024 employees and 156 managers from 44 tour operators. A scale is a set of items measuring one concept.So this means: two sources (employees and managers) reduce the risk that one person's view colours everything.→ Next: the analysis.

⭐ With one model that tests all links at once while respecting that employees sit in teams; it checks the route (mediation) and the leader's amplifying role (moderation).Multilevel structural equation modeling estimates all paths at once and respects that employees are nested in teams. An indirect effect is the effect that runs through the mediator; bootstrapping estimates its uncertainty by resampling the data. Simple slopes show the effect of HR at high and low levels of the leader variable.So this means: the analysis matches the multilevel model: individuals in teams.→ Next: what we may conclude.

⭐ Relationships, not causes: two waves and two sources reduce the risk of common method bias, but it was not a cross-lagged design.Common method bias is distortion when everything is measured the same way at the same time. A cross-lagged design measures all variables at several times to see which predicts which. The route and the outcomes were measured at the same moment (crafting and individual OCBE both from employees at time 2). A marker variable test (an unrelated question added to check for distortion) suggested a low risk of common method bias. The author states that causal relationships might not be derived.So this means: an exam option saying the two waves show green HR causes crafting is wrong.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ A two-wave, two-source survey of Vietnamese tour teams, analysed with multilevel SEM; it shows relationships, not causes.1Sample? Tour departments in Ho Chi Minh City; snowball sampling.2Measures? Five scales; collective OCBE by managers.3Analysis? Multilevel SEM; bootstrapped indirect effects; simple slopes.4Conclusion limits? Not cross-lagged; no causal claims.Typical exam questions on this step:?Why can the study not claim causality? (not cross-lagged; survey design)?Who rated collective OCBE? (the managers)

⚠ The trap✗ Because the survey had two waves, the study shows that green HR practices cause green crafting.✓ Two waves separate the measurements in time, which helps against common method bias. But it was not a cross-lagged study, and the author states that causal relationships might not be derived.

The author's conclusion (p. 1186), in our words. Green HR practices were positively related to organizational citizenship behavior for the environment of individuals and of teams, with collective green crafting as the mediator. Environmentally specific servant leadership made the positive relationships of green HR practices with collective green crafting and with citizenship behavior at both levels stronger.

🧰 What organizations should do, according to the author

Implement green HR practices to reach the green goals. Through green training, give employees green-related resources: awareness and values, other-oriented values, and the knowledge and skills for green activities and eco-initiatives such as eco tours or destination greening programs. Include employee involvement activities that give team members autonomy, so that they can push the green agenda themselves and craft resources for green activities together. Ensure consistency in signals from HR practices and managers. Build environmentally specific servant leadership across the organization through leadership training and succession planning, and let managers share their experience with it in training sessions or through internal communication.

⚠️ Limitations the author names

No cross-lagged design, so causal relationships might not be derived. Data based on participants' perceptions, which may not reflect what actually happens; future studies could use HR department reports for the practices and participant observation for OCBE. Self-reported questionnaires carry a risk of common method bias, although the author judges it not a serious concern here because of the marker variable test, the moderation tests and the two sources. Results from tourism in Vietnam may not generalize to other industries and markets; the collectivistic nature of Vietnamese culture might influence the degree of team green behavior, so the model should be compared in collectivistic and individualistic cultures.

🔍 Three things that do not quite add up (our observation)

The article says the time lag separates independent, mediator and dependent variables, but the mediator and the outcomes were both measured at time 2, and crafting and individual OCBE came from the same employees at the same moment. The reported shares of variance at team and at employee level do not add up to 100%. And the article sometimes uses causal words such as impact and enhanced, although its own limitations say causality cannot be derived. None of this changes the numbers above; it is a reason to describe the results as relationships.

🧩 The meeting's questions, from this reading

What is green human resource management? HRM activities that improve environmental outcomes (Kramar, 2014): recruitment and selection, training, performance management, rewards and involvement, aligned with green objectives. What is organizational citizenship behavior for the environment? Voluntary behavior, not in the job description, that helps make the organization or society more sustainable; for a team, the synergy of members' voluntary green efforts; in three forms: eco-civic engagement, eco-helping and eco-initiatives. How can organizations increase pro-environmental behaviors? This study's answer: green HR practices, room for teams to craft their green work, and leaders who send the same green signal. For the group assignment on a company-wide initiative, the article offers material for all three parts it asks for: policies (green HR practices such as training and involvement), employee incentives (rewards are one of the five practices: financial and non-financial, such as green recognition; the managerial section itself stresses involvement and autonomy), and leadership (environmentally specific servant leadership, built through leadership training and succession planning).
