Leadership and environmental sustainability

Zacher, H., Kühner, C., Katz, I. M., & Rudolph, C. W. (2024). Leadership and environmental sustainability: An integrative conceptual model of multilevel antecedents and consequences of leader green behavior. Group & Organization Management, 49(2), 365–394.

STEP1
Who wrote it, and which problem they want to solve

"Green leadership" mixes up three things

❓ This step answers: What do the authors find wrong with earlier research on leadership and green behavior, and what do they set out to do?
Authors
Hannes Zacher and Clara Kühner (Leipzig University), Ian M. Katz (Old Dominion University) and Cort W. Rudolph (Wayne State University). Three of them wrote reading 1
Type
Conceptual paper in Group & Organization Management (2024): it reports no new data. It reviews studies, defines concepts and proposes a model
Question
How should leadership for environmental sustainability be defined, and how do its causes and consequences hang together across levels?
Main message
Study what leaders do (leader green behavior), not style labels; it is the link between causes and results at the levels of organization, leader and followers

What do the authors find wrong with earlier research on leadership and green behavior, and what do they set out to do?Why this matters. Reading 1 told you that leadership goes with employee green behavior, with a moderate link. This conceptual paper (building ideas from existing research, without new data) asks what that "leadership" actually was. It answers the theme's question about the role of leadership.In this step you learn:1The problem with leadership style labels2The two limits of the literature3The three goals of the article

⭐ A style label bundles the leader's behavior with the good results it is assumed to produce, so you cannot tell what the leader actually does.A leadership style is a pattern of leader behaviors named with an adjective: transformational (in our words, inspiring with a vision), servant, ethical. A meta-analysis found positive, moderate links of green transformational leadership, servant leadership and supervisory support with employees' green behavior (Katz and colleagues, 2022). The authors value this work but ask what was measured: an "adjective leadership" style already contains the leader's assumed intentions and outcomes. One definition even called environmental leadership "the ability to influence individuals and mobilize organizations to realize a vision of long-term ecological sustainability" (Egri & Herman, 2000, quoted on p. 374). The authors point at the word ability: in our reading, it names an antecedent (an ability) and a result (sustainability), but no behavior. Our example: calling a manager a "great coach" already says people improve, without saying what she does.So this means: an exam option saying the authors show green transformational leadership does not work is wrong; their point is what the label measures.→ Next: the two limits.

⭐ Style labels that conflate behavior with its antecedents and consequences, and fragmentation.1Conflation: broad, positive-sounding style labels mix the content of leader behavior with its possible causes or results (the term "adjective leadership" is from Day & Antonakis, 2013; the criticism from Fischer & Sitkin, 2023).2Fragmentation: every study uses its own mix of leadership constructs, mediators (links in the middle), moderators (factors that change how strong a link is) and levels, so findings do not add up.So this means: what is needed: clear definitions, causes and results kept apart at several levels, and one integrating model.→ Next: what the authors do about it.

⭐ Define leadership and leader green behavior, present an integrative model with eight propositions, and give research recommendations.(1) Define leadership for environmental sustainability and leader green behavior, by combining the behavioral approach to leadership (studying what leaders actually do, not labels) with reading 1's theory of employee green behavior. (2) Present a model of antecedents and consequences of leader green behavior at the levels of leader, followers and organization, with eight propositions; its structure is borrowed from a model of leadership and employee health (Wegge and colleagues, 2014). (3) Recommend future research. Their two contributions: applying the six forms of green behavior from reading 1 (the green six) to leaders, and positioning leader green behavior as the link between causes and results at three levels.So this means: the article replaces a vague label with an observable behavior at the centre of a model.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Style labels mix behavior with assumed causes and results; the authors define leader green behavior and build a multilevel model around it.1What is wrong with styles? They conflate behavior with assumed antecedents and outcomes.2Two limits? Conflation and fragmentation.3Goals? Definitions, an integrative model with eight propositions, recommendations.Typical exam questions on this step:?A researcher defines green leadership as "the capacity to make teams environmentally friendly". Which criticism applies? (it names an antecedent and a consequence, no behavior)?Which statement is wrong? "The authors show green transformational leadership does not work."

STEP3
The two definitions the article stands on

Leadership as a process, leader green behavior as actions

❓ This step answers: How do the authors define leadership in the context of environmental sustainability, and what is leader green behavior?

How do the authors define leadership in the context of environmental sustainability, and what is leader green behavior?Why this matters. Step 1 showed why style labels fail. These two definitions replace them, and the whole model in steps 4 to 6 stands on them.In this step you learn:1Leadership as a process2Leader green behavior as actions3The green six and the three lines for leaders4Why a behavior beats a style

⭐ A process in which leaders use their actions to steer followers and/or the organization toward environmental sustainability goals that are more or less shared.A process unfolds between people over time, unlike a property of one person. The authors build on a general definition of leadership (Judge & Long, 2012). Environmental sustainability means dealing responsibly with the natural environment so that quality of life on Earth can continue. Notice: it is a process, not a trait; the target can be followers or the organization (for example a new policy); and the goals need only be "more or less" shared.So this means: leadership here is something leaders do with others, not something they are.→ Next: the behavior itself.

⭐ The things a leader does on purpose, that can be rated for how well they are done, and that help or harm environmental sustainability, directly or indirectly.Leader green behavior (also leader pro-environmental behavior) is built on reading 1's definition. The actions are under the leader's control. Direct: the leader's own act touches the environment (for example recycling, or wasting resources). Indirect: through changes in the context (new policies or work tasks), in follower behavior (orders, inspirational motivation, behavioral modeling) or in organizational behavior (influence on environmental investment decisions).So this means: an exam option calling leader green behavior only the "influencing others" part is wrong.→ Next: how to classify it.

⭐ By the same map as employees: the green six dimensions and three lines (in-role or extra-role, direct or indirect, low or high intensity).The green six: the Green Five plus counterproductive sustainability behaviors. In-role: part of the leader's duties; extra-role: voluntary. Low intensity: little effort or risk; high intensity: more effort and risk. The article's example: using reusable cups in team meetings is low intensity; persuading upper management to adopt a new sustainability policy is high intensity. Green behaviors can be performed at different levels of the hierarchy (Ones and colleagues, 2018), and by people without an official role: shared or emergent leadership. Our example: a plant manager who changes the team's machine schedule so machines run on solar power changes the work context: indirect leader green behavior (transforming).So this means: the same map used for employees now describes leaders.→ Next: why this beats a style.

⭐ Because it names actions only, covers what leaders do themselves and in the context, and keeps causes and results outside the definition.A style only describes how a leader influences followers. The green six also cover the leader's own direct behavior and changes in the work and organizational context. Because antecedents and consequences stay outside, they can be studied separately.So this means: a clean definition is the precondition for the model.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Leadership is a process toward shared environmental goals; leader green behavior is the leader's own scalable actions, classified with the green six and three lines.1Leadership? A process steering followers and/or the organization toward sustainability goals.2Leader green behavior? Purposeful, scalable actions that help or harm the environment, directly or indirectly.3Classification? Green six, three lines; also emergent leaders.4Better than a style? Actions only; includes own and contextual behavior; causes and results apart.Typical exam questions on this step:?Classify: a plant manager reschedules machines for solar power (indirect, transforming)?Which statement is wrong? "Leader green behavior is the influencing-others dimension."

⚠ The trap✗ Leader green behavior is the "influencing others" part of the Green Five.✓ It is all six dimensions. Influencing others is one of them. A leader who recycles, redesigns a process or flies unnecessarily also shows leader green behavior.
STEP4
The model, part 1 · Figure 1 · propositions 1 to 3

What leads to leader green behavior

❓ This step answers: According to the model, what makes a leader show green behavior?

According to the model, what makes a leader show green behavior?Why this matters. The model has leader green behavior at its centre. This step covers its left side: propositions 1 to 3, the causes. A proposition is a statement the authors propose and research still has to test.In this step you learn:1How Figure 1 is organized2Proposition 1: the leader's own determinants3Proposition 2: followers influence the leader4Proposition 3: the context, and two theories about interaction

⭐ In our redrawing of Figure 1: as a grid of levels (organization, leader, followers) and columns (antecedents, green behavior, consequences), with eight propositions in three groups.Multilevel means layered: organization highest, then the leader, then followers. In Figure 1, dark arrows are effects of or on leader green behavior; light arrows are effects among other constructs; solid arrows are more-established propositions and dashed ones less-established. The groups: what leads to leader green behavior (1–3), how it reaches followers and context (4–5), and what it brings (6–8).So this means: read the arrows' style to see how solid each proposition is.→ Next: proposition 1.

⭐ Leader green behavior is primarily influenced by knowledge, skills and motivation; abilities, values, personality, attitudes, beliefs and affect work through them.This applies reading 1's performance theory to leaders. Motivation means choices about the direction, intensity and duration of effort. Parts are supported by studies; the chain as a whole is new and untested. Our example: a leader who cares about nature still needs to know which suppliers are greener before she switches.So this means: values matter, but only through knowing, being able and wanting.→ Next: a bottom-up effect.

⭐ Followers influence their leader, indirectly by educating, persuading or encouraging, and directly because leaders may model what followers do.This is a bottom-up effect, in our view the most unusual proposition. Followers' own "influencing others" behavior changes the leader's antecedents; and leaders may observe and copy followers' green or counterproductive behavior. The article's example: followers petitioning for hybrid work to cut commuting emissions. The one supporting study (Javaid and colleagues, 2023) found that followers' active engagement and critical thinking went with more leader green behavior; it did not test the mechanisms.So this means: influence runs up as well as down.→ Next: the context.

⭐ The context influences leader green behavior indirectly via leader antecedents, directly, and by changing how strongly those antecedents matter.Context: strategy, initiatives, culture, climate, equipment, materials, technology and work design. Evidence for the context in general: meta-analytic links of corporate social responsibility, green HRM, perceived organizational support and green psychological climate with green behavior, and studies showing an environmental policy goes with more green behavior. Only one study linked context to leader antecedents (Cantor and colleagues, 2015); Rubel and colleagues (2021) found green HRM went with more leader green behavior via green work climate; for part (c) there is one cross-sectional study (the link between managers' perceived behavioral control and green citizenship was stronger when social norms were high; Akterujjaman and colleagues, 2022). Part (c), the interaction, rests on two theories. An interaction means the strength of one effect depends on something else.So this means: organizations shape leaders three ways: through them, on them, and by turning their traits up or down.→ Next: the two theories.

⭐ A strong situation makes the leader's own motivation matter less; a trait-activating context makes it show more.Situation strength: a clear policy that limits discretion reduces the role of personal differences. In a weak situation (more freedom in how to act), the leader's own motivation matters more. Trait activation: a context that invites a trait, such as a green office design, lets it show. The article presents them as complementary.So this means: an exam option saying the two theories predict the same thing is wrong.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Leader green behavior comes from the leader's knowledge, skills and motivation (1), from followers (2) and from the context, which can also strengthen or weaken the leader's own drivers (3).1Model? Levels × columns; solid = established, dashed = less established.2P1? Knowledge, skills, motivation; other traits work through them.3P2? Followers influence leaders (bottom-up).4P3? Context: indirect, direct, and moderating.5Two theories? Strong situations dampen, trait activation amplifies.Typical exam questions on this step:?What is the bottom-up effect in proposition 2??Situation strength vs trait activation: which makes motivation matter less? (situation strength)

⚠ The trap✗ Situation strength theory and trait activation theory predict the same thing.✓ Both are about interaction, and the article presents them as complementary. A strong situation (a clear policy that limits discretion) makes the leader's own motivation matter less. A trait-activating context (a green office design) makes it show more.
STEP5
The model, part 2 · propositions 4 and 5

How a leader's green behavior reaches followers

❓ This step answers: What is the role of leadership in promoting employee green behavior: by which pathways does it work?

What is the role of leadership in promoting employee green behavior: by which pathways does it work?Why this matters. This is where the model answers the question most studies asked. Instead of one arrow, the authors propose several pathways, borrowed from a model of leadership and employee health. They call propositions 4 and 5 reverse pathways: from the leader back to followers and context.In this step you learn:1Person-focused action and modeling (4a, 4b)2Changing the strength of follower drivers (4c)3System-focused and moderating action (5a, 5b)

⭐ Through person-focused action: training, instructing, persuading and encouraging, which improves followers' knowledge, skills and motivation (4a).The study: Robertson and Barling (2013) found green transformational leadership and leader green behavior went with more employee environmental passion, which went with more employee green behavior. Our example: a team leader who shows staff how to set printers to double-sided and explains why it matters.So this means: the leader works on what followers know, can and want.→ Next: a second route.

⭐ Followers observe and copy the leader's green or counterproductive behavior (4b).This is behavioral modeling: leaders may serve a powerful role-model function. The study: Shao and colleagues (2023), drawing on social learning theory, studied the trickle-down effect of leader green behavior on follower green behavior. What they found: leader green role-model influence and then followers' green self-efficacy carried the link. In plain words: followers who see their leader act green feel more able to act green themselves. Because all data came from one time point (cross-sectional), this shows an association, not proof of cause. The same route can carry harm: a leader's counterproductive behavior can be copied too.So this means: an exam option saying trickle-down means the leader tells followers what to do is wrong: telling is person-focused action.→ Next: a subtler route.

⭐ Yes: by changing how strongly followers' own antecedents lead to their behavior (4c).This follows situation strength and trait activation theories, as in step 4. The study: Graves and colleagues (2013) found that employees' external motivation went with more green behavior when environmental transformational leadership was high, and less when it was low. Our example: a leader who makes green choices easy lets a motivated follower's motivation actually show in behavior.So this means: leaders can turn followers' own green drivers up or down.→ Next: the system around followers.

⭐ By changing policies, practices and work design (system-focused action, 5a), and by shaping how organizational policies land in the team (moderating action, 5b).System-focused action: changing contextual antecedents, which indirectly influences followers. Moderating action: strengthening or softening the impact of contextual factors, for example deciding whether and how to implement a new performance management policy that includes sustainability. Leaders change the system through the transforming, avoiding-harm and taking-initiative dimensions of the green six; green HRM had stronger effects when supervisory support for the environment was high (Liu and colleagues, 2023).So this means: leaders act on the system as well as on people.→ Next: what this means for a leader.

⭐ A wider repertoire than "be inspiring": teach and encourage, do it yourself visibly, change policies and work design, and manage how policies land.Our summary; the article itself draws conclusions for research, not for practice.So this means: leadership for sustainability has five routes (4a, 4b, 4c, 5a, 5b), and each can also carry harm.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Leaders reach followers through person-focused action, modeling and changing the strength of follower drivers (P4), and through system-focused and moderating action (P5).1Person-focused? Train, instruct, persuade, encourage (4a).2Modeling? Followers copy the leader's behavior (4b; Shao et al., 2023).3Strength of drivers? 4c: situation strength, trait activation.4System? 5a policies and work design; 5b how policies land.5Practice? Five routes; harm can travel them too.Typical exam questions on this step:?What is the role of leadership in promoting employee green behavior? (five pathways: person-focused action, modeling, changing the strength of follower drivers, system-focused action, moderating action)?Which statement is wrong? "Trickle-down means telling followers what to do."

⚠ The trap✗ Trickle-down means the leader tells followers what to do.✓ In the article, the trickle-down effect studied by Shao and colleagues (2023) is about modeling: followers see the leader's own green behavior, which works through role model influence and their green self-efficacy. Telling and training is person-focused action.

What leader green behavior brings, and what we actually know

❓ This step answers: Which consequences does the model propose for leaders, followers and the organization, and how much evidence is there?

Which consequences does the model propose for leaders, followers and the organization, and how much evidence is there?Why this matters. The right-hand column holds the consequences, one box per level. Read every sentence here as "the authors propose", and check how much has been studied.In this step you learn:1Proposition 6: the leader2Proposition 7: followers3Proposition 8: the organization4How solid each one is

⭐ Leader green behavior influences the leader's need satisfaction, environmental performance and leadership success, depending on fit with the leader's values and the organization's values.Leader environmental performance means, for example, lower personal resource use and waste; need satisfaction may then raise well-being. The condition is congruence (fit). Green behavior satisfies needs when it fits the leader's environmental values, and earns favorable evaluations of leadership success when the organization values sustainability. No study has examined this yet.So this means: an exam option saying the article shows green leaders are happier and more successful is wrong.→ Next: followers.

⭐ It influences followers' consequences, such as need satisfaction, and how strongly followers' green behavior pays off for them.Part (b) means: whether acting green pays off for a follower depends on the leader, who can support and reward it, or penalize its absence. In Figure 1 the follower consequences are need satisfaction and individual and team environmental performance. Only two studies looked at part (a): green leadership went with higher follower well-being (Iqbal & Piwowar-Sulej, 2022) and lower turnover intentions (Li and colleagues, 2021). No study has tested part (b).So this means: the leader decides whether followers' green efforts are rewarded.→ Next: the organization.

⭐ It influences organizational environmental performance and attractiveness, directly and through leader and follower outcomes, and changes how strongly contextual factors pay off for the organization.Indirect contributions through propositions 6 and 7 follow the bottom-up logic of emergence (individual results adding up to organizational ones). The study for part (b): Zhang and Ma (2021). What they found: high environmental leadership strengthened the link between environmental management practices and green innovation performance.So this means: leaders can make the organization's green practices pay off more.→ Next: how much we know.

⭐ Among the consequences: most for the organization, very little for followers, and nothing yet for leaders.Note: the 143 studies on follower green behavior from step 2 are about the pathways in step 5, not about consequences. Several studies cover firms' environmental performance; very few cover follower well-being or turnover intentions; none cover leader outcomes or the interaction in 7b.So this means: the model's right side is mostly a research agenda.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Proposed consequences: for the leader (6, conditional, unstudied), followers (7) and the organization (8, the best studied).1P6? Leader outcomes, depending on value fit; no studies.2P7? STEP7
The authors' recommendations for future research

How to study leader green behavior properly

❓ This step answers: What do the authors recommend for theory and for methods?

What do the authors recommend for theory and for methods?Why this matters. The article ends with a research agenda. It doubles as a checklist for reading every other study in this theme.In this step you learn:1Recommendations for theory2Three extensions of the model3Recommendations for methods

⭐ Treat leadership as a process, define leader green behavior as actions, pause style labels, and study the micro-dynamics.A moratorium is a pause: here, stop developing and using adjective style labels. Study how leader antecedents interact with follower behavior and context, for example with action regulation theory (goals, orientation, planning, execution, feedback, and habits). Examine whether one higher-order leader green behavior factor sits above the six dimensions.So this means: the authors ask the field to stop measuring labels and start measuring actions.→ Next: how to widen the model.

⭐ By adding the societal and cultural level, team and within-person processes, and leader–follower fit.1Society and culture: national regulations, traditions and values, such as Schwartz's self-transcendence (in our words, caring beyond yourself: universalism, concern for all people and nature, and benevolence, concern for people close to you).2Team and within-person processes: coworker advocacy, team climate, and how leader and follower green behavior fluctuate over time. The model treats one leader's followers as a team.3Fit: what happens when leader and followers differ or clash, how leaders deal with followers who resist or boycott, and how conflicting messages limit the leader's influence.So this means: the model is a start; culture, time and conflict are next.→ Next: methods.

⭐ Measure the behavior itself, from several sources and over time, because single surveys and outcome data each distort it and only experiments can show cause.Surveys measure perceptions of behavior, open to memory distortion and self-enhancement. Video observation is more accurate but costly, and people act differently when watched (demand effects). Several sources avoid inflated links from common method bias; cross-sectional designs cannot test causal or mediation claims. Also sample several hierarchical levels and compare predictors at different levels in one study. Objective measures such as energy savings measure outcomes, which may not be fully under a person's control; the authors treat them as indicators of consequences, to combine with other sources. Testing incremental contribution means showing leader green behavior predicts beyond established leadership constructs.So this means: an exam option saying objective energy data are the best measure of green behavior is wrong: they measure outcomes.→ Next: a checklist for you.

⭐ Ask of any study: was it a style or a behavior, one moment or several, one source or several, and was anything manipulated?Our use of the recommendations: apply these four questions to the other readings in this theme.So this means: the recommendations make you a sharper reader of every study.→ Next: check what you can now answer.

⭐ Theory: actions not labels, micro-dynamics, extensions to culture, time and fit. Methods: know your measures, multiple sources and waves, experiments, levels, added value.1Theory? Process view; moratorium on styles; micro-dynamics; higher-order factor.2Extensions? Society and culture; team and within-person; leader–follower fit.3Methods? Measures, sources, time, experiments, levels, incremental value.4Checklist? (they are outcomes)

The authors' conclusion (p. 387), in our words. The authors expect a great deal from leadership when it comes to making organizations environmentally sustainable. 🧠 Why this matters

Reading 1 told you that leadership goes with employee green behavior. This reading opens that box. It says "leadership" was measured loosely, replaces it with a behavior you can observe, and lays out the routes in both directions: from leader to followers and context, and from followers and context back to the leader. That two-way picture is what makes it a systemic model.

🧩 The learning question, answered from this reading

What is the role of leadership in promoting employee green behavior? Leaders promote it by person-focused action (training, instructing, persuading, encouraging), by behavioral modeling (their own green behavior is copied), by system-focused action (changing policies, practices and work design) and by moderating action (deciding how contextual factors land), and they can make followers' own attitudes and motivation count more or less. The evidence: a moderate positive association between green leadership styles and employee green behavior in a meta-analysis, and many single studies on mediators and moderators; the pathways as defined here are propositions. Leaders are in turn influenced by their own knowledge, skills and motivation, by their followers and by the organization.

🔗 Connection

A note on words: the authors use "pro-environmental", "environmentally-specific" and "green" as synonyms. And the triple bottom line from reading 1 is also known as the three P's: people, planet, profits. Next to environmental sustainability stand social sustainability (an organization's contributions to social well-being, including equity and inclusion) and economic sustainability (its contributions to economic development and growth); this article is only about the environmental part. Looking ahead: reading 5 (Luu, 2019) studies environmentally specific servant leadership together with green human resource practices, which is exactly the kind of "adjective style" study this article reflects on. Reading 6 is about employees who proactively drive green initiatives; keep in mind what this article calls emergent leadership when you get there.
